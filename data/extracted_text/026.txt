In-plane current-induced magnetization switching in CoGa/MnGa/MgO ﬁlms
Masahiro Takikawa1,2, Kazuya Z. Suzuki1*, Reza Ranjbar1, and Shigemi Mizukami1
1WPI Advanced Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, Japan
2Department of Applied Physics, Graduate School of Engineering, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8579, Japan
*E-mail: kazuya.suzuki.d8@tohoku.ac.jp
Received May 9, 2017; accepted May 26, 2017; published online June 22, 2017
In-plane current-induced magnetization switching was investigated in a micron-sized Hall bar for the paramagnetic CoGa-buffered and MgO-
capped 2-nm-thick ferrimagnetic L10 MnGa layer with perpendicular magnetic anisotropy. Current-induced switching was clearly observed when
the in-plane longitudinal magnetic ﬁeld applied was sufﬁciently large and at an injection current density of 1011 A/m2. The in-plane ﬁeld direction
and CoGa thickness dependence indicate that the deterministic bipolar switching primarily originates from a damping-like spin–orbit torque (SOT)
arising from the spin-Hall effect in CoGa with a positive spin-Hall angle. The current pulse response suggested that the SOT switching was
thermally assisted by Joule heating. © 2017 The Japan Society of Applied Physics
C
urrent-induced spin–orbit torque (SOT) switching of
magnetization in ﬁlms with perpendicular magnetic
anisotropy (PMA) is of importance for a writing
technique for a three-terminal device utilizing a perpendic-
ularly magnetized magnetic tunnel junction (p-MTJ), which
can serve as a memory element of magnetoresistive random
access memory (MRAM).1–6) Most studies on SOT for PMA
ﬁlms have been performed on thin layered ﬁlms with an
interfacial PMA composed of a heavy metal, e.g., Ta and
Pt, and ultrathin ferromagnetic metals and alloys, e.g., Co
and CoFeB. Moreover, SOT for magnetic alloys and multi-
layers with a bulk-like PMA are of interest in determining
whether MRAM using SOT switching can be scaled below
the 20-nm technology node, which would require a large
PMA thickness product of Keff
u d > 1 mJ=m2 to maintain the
long retention.7–9)
Tetragonal Heusler-like Mn-based alloys and their deriv-
atives, such as L10 MnGa, D022 Mn3Ga, and D022 Mn3Ge,
have been extensively studied as magnetic materials for
MRAM applications. This is because these Mn-based mate-
rials have very low net magnetic moments owing to ferri-
magnetism,10) high PMA,11–14) low Gilbert damping,12,15) and
high spin polarization,10,13,16,17) which are rarely seen in other
materials in bulk form. We recently discovered the room
temperature (RT) growth of 1- to 3-nm L10 MnGa ultrathin
ﬁlms utilizing the paramagnetic B2 ordered CoGa buﬀer
layer.18) By using this method, we demonstrated ultrathin
MnGa=MgO p-MTJs19) and also the growth of (001)-textured
polycrystalline MnGa ﬁlms.20) In addition, we have observed
clear SOT switching for CoGa=MnGa=Pt ﬁlms,21) though
another group studied a GaAs=MnGa=heavy metal layer.22,23)
However, a stacking structure with a heavy metal capping
layer is not useful for p-MTJs in practice.
In this Letter, we report the observation of current-induced
SOT magnetization switching in CoGa=MnGa=MgO ﬁlms
even without the heavy metal capping layer on the MnGa
layer. The ﬁlms were fabricated by ultrahigh vacuum mag-
netron sputtering and the stacking structure was (001) MgO
single-crystal substrate=MgO (10)=CoGa (15, 20, and 25)=
MnGa (2)=Mg (0.4)=MgO (2)=Ta (2) (where the thicknesses
are in nanometers) [Fig. 1(a)]. The ﬂushing of MgO substrate
was performed at 700 °C and the CoGa layer was annealed
in situ at 500 °C. All the layers were deposited at RT. The
ﬁlm compositions of CoGa and MnGa were Co55Ga45 and
Mn59Ga41, respectively, as estimated by inductively coupled
plasma mass spectroscopy. X-ray diﬀraction measurements
showed that the B2-ordered paramagnetic CoGa buﬀer with
the (001) orientation was epitaxially grown on the MgO
substrate. The top Ta layer serves as a layer protecting MgO
from reacting with the air and has no eﬀect on the SOT, as
mentioned later. The ﬁlm was patterned into the micron-sized
Hall bar by conventional ultraviolet photolithography and
Ar ion milling, as studied before.21) The length and width of
the main Hall bar are 30 and 6 µm, respectively [Fig. 1(b)],
and the resistances R were approximately 142, 104, and 88 Ω
for samples with dCoGa = 15, 20, and 25 nm. Hereafter, the
dCoGa = 20 nm sample is described. Most of the current ﬂows
in the CoGa layer since a thin Ta layer may be partially
oxidized and the resistivity of MnGa is ≃1.6 µΩ·m,24) which
is comparable to that of CoGa (≃2.5 µΩ·m).25) The anom-
alous Hall resistance RH was measured at RT by measuring
the voltage across the side Hall bars while injecting current I.

mechanism of the spin-Hall angle is still under discussion
even in elemental metals, such as Pt. A recent study has
suggested that the spin-Hall angle for Pt linearly increases
with increasing resistivity;28) B2 CoGa has been known to
have a high resistivity at a composition deviating from the
stoichiometry and its resistivity reached a maximum at 55%
Co in the bulk study.25) Moreover, the spin–orbit interaction
for Ga 4p orbitals is ξSO = 155 meV, which is comparable to
that for Pd 4d orbitals (= 189 meV).29) Pd-based layered
systems, such as Co=Pd multilayers, Pd=L10 FePd, and
Pd=Co=AlOx, have also exhibited relatively large SOT7,8) and
current-induced switching.30) Therefore, it is likely that
highly resistive B2 CoGa also has a relatively large spin-
Hall angle. An investigation of the precise value of eff
SH for
CoGa remains as an open subject for future study.
In
summary,
in-plane
current-induced
magnetization
switching was investigated in CoGa=MnGa=MgO ﬁlms.
Current-induced switching was clearly observed at a current
density of 1011 A=m2. The in-plane ﬁeld direction and CoGa
thickness dependence indicated that damping-like SOT
resulting from the spin-Hall eﬀect in CoGa with positive
spin-Hall angle is the main cause of the deterministic bipolar
switching. This study demonstrated that SOT switching is
possible even in a CoGa=MnGa=MgO structure serving as
the bottom electrode for p-MTJs.
