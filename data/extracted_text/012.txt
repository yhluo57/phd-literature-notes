ABSTRACT
We studied MgO barrier magnetic tunnel junctions (MTJs) comprising perpendicularly magnetized MnGa and FeCoB electrodes. In those
perpendicular (p-) MTJs, we utilized thin metastable bcc CoMn alloys as an interlayer between MnGa and MgO to enhance the tun-
nel magnetoresistance (TMR) effect. Moreover, we investigated the effect of a thin Mg interlayer between MnGa and CoMn. Owing to
the interlayer engineering, we achieved a TMR ratio over 100%, the highest value observed for p-MTJs with a tetragonal MnGa elec-
trode. Our study contributes to the further development of spintronic devices using p-MTJs with various Mn-based tetragonal alloy
electrodes.

I. INTRODUCTION
Tetragonal Mn-based ordered alloy thin films, such as MnZ
(Z = Al, Ga), Mn3Z (Z = Al, Ga, Ge, Sn), and MnYZ (Y = Al,
Ga, Mn, Z = Ge, Sb), tend to have large perpendicular anisotropy
(PMA), small magnetization, and a relatively low Gilbert damping
constant.1–5 These unique magnetic properties are well suited for
magnetic electrodes in perpendicular (p-) magnetic tunnel junctions
(MTJs), which can be used for magnetoresistive memory (MRAM)
and potential logic and high-frequency applications.6–8 To date,
extensive studies have been conducted on those Mn-based alloy thin
films from various perspectives.9–22
A challenging issue is to obtain a large TMR ratio in p-
MTJs with Mn-based alloy electrodes. First-principles calculations
have suggested TMR ratios in percentages of hundreds in the p-
MTJ comprising a (001)-oriented MgO barrier and Mn-based alloy
electrodes.23 However, such significant TMR effects have not been
experimentally observed.24–26 Furthermore, p-MTJs with Mn-based
alloy electrodes generally exhibit a small TMR effect, despite using
an interlayer, such as FeCo(B) (001), between MgO(001) and (001)-
oriented Mn-based alloys.27–29 It contrasts with the high TMR
ratio above 100% typically obtained in p-MTJ with a MgO(001)
barrier and FeCo(B) (001) electrodes.30 Moreover, a much higher
TMR ratio, up to ∼600%, is obtained with in-plane magnetized
MTJs.31–34
Recently, we investigated p-MTJs comprising MnGa(001)/
MgO(001)/FeCo(B) (001) and reported a TMR ratio of 85% when
using a metastable bcc CoMn(001) interlayer between MnGa
and MgO.35 Our TMR ratio was relatively large compared with
that previously reported for a similar p-MTJ using a CoFe(001)

metastable bcc CoMn alloys.36–38 Therefore, it may be valuable to
investigate the p-MTJ with a CoMn interlayer to enhance the TMR
effect from a technical viewpoint.
A key material in these p-MTJs is the metastable bcc CoMn
interlayer, which may be stabilized on the MnGa (001) surface with
epitaxial strain.39,40 The magnetic and electronic properties of bcc
CoMn are sensitive to the composition and local atomic structures
of Co and Mn and the axial ratio of the bcc cell, as discussed in
thick films on a Cr(001) buffer.39 The thermodynamically stable
phase of CoMn is fcc or hcp and exhibits ferrimagnetism or antifer-
romagnetism depending on the composition owing to the antifer-
romagnetic coupling of Mn.41,42 Even in the bcc metastable phase,
an antiferromagnetic pairing of Mn atoms occupying the nearest
neighbor sites influences their electronic structure and magnetism.39
With an interlayer, the local crystal structures and compositions
may also be influenced by the MnGa(001) surface conditions and
CoMn/MnGa interface abruptness, eventually affecting the TMR
effect.
To gain insight into the aforementioned issue, we investigated
the effect of an ultrathin Mg interlayer between MnGa and CoMn
as a case study. Such an interlayer can modify the nanostructure of
the CoMn interlayer in p-MTJs. The Mg interlayer does not disturb
the epitaxial growth of bcc CoMn(001) on MnGa(001) when the Mg
interlayer thickness is appropriate. Moreover, it increases the TMR
ratio by more than 100% in the p-MTJs.
II. EXPERIMENTAL METHODS
MTJ
stacking
films
were
prepared
using
magnetron
sputtering with a base pressure below 1 × 10−7 Pa.35 The stacking
structure was as follows: MgO(001) substrate/Cr(40)/Co55Ga45(30)/
Mn61Ga39(3)/Mg (tMg = 0–0.8)/Co80Mn20(tCoMn = 0.8–1.4)/Mg(0.4)
/MgO(2)/Fe60Co20B20(1.4)/Ta(3)/Ru(5) (thickness in nm). The
Co80Mn20 interlayer was deposited via a co-sputtering technique
using pure Co (99.9%) and Mn (99.99%) targets. All layers were
deposited
without
intentional
heating.
We
also
fabricated
films with MgO(001) substrate/Co55Ga45(15)/Mn61Ga39(3)/Mg(0.4)/
Co80Mn20(tCoMn = 0.8–1.4)/MgO (2)/Ta(1.5) stacking for anoma-
lous Hall effect (AHE) measurements. Microfabrication of MTJs
was performed using standard ultraviolet photolithography and
Ar ion milling. MTJs with junction areas of 10 × 10, 20 × 20, and
30 × 30 μm2 were obtained. The MTJs were annealed in a vacuum
furnace without an external magnetic field. The magnetoresistance
(MR) measurements were performed at room temperature using
a four-probe technique with a maximum magnetic field of 5 kOe.
We also used a physical property measurement system (PPMS,
Quantum Design) for the MR and AHE measurements at different
temperatures under a high magnetic field. Structural characteri-
zation of the MTJ cross-section was performed using high-angle
annular dark field scanning transmission electron microscopy
(HAADF-STEM) and energy dispersive x-ray (EDX) spectroscopy.

Figure 1(b) shows the MTJ annealing temperature dependence
of the TMR ratio in p-MTJs with different Mg interlayer thicknesses.
The maximum TMR ratio is observed at annealing temperatures
between 250 and 275 ○C in all cases. The maximum values of the
TMR ratio as a function of the Mg interlayer thickness and the
corresponding resistance-area products in a parallel state of magne-
tization (RAp) are depicted in Fig. 1(c). The TMR ratio increases as
the Mg interlayer thickness increases from 0 to 0.5 nm [Fig. 1(c)].
The Mg interlayer maximizes the TMR effect for thicknesses of
0.4 and 0.5 nm; however, the corresponding RAp values are lower
[Fig. 1(c)]. In contrast, when the Mg interlayer is between 0.6 and
0.8 nm, the TMR ratio decreases while the RAp increases rapidly.
Therefore, the formation of bcc CoMn(001) and the lattice coher-
ence of CoMn(001)/MgO(001)/FeCo(B) (001) may be preserved
for Mg interlayer thickness of 0–0.5 nm at annealing temperatures
below 250–275 ○C. A significant change in crystalline structures and
orientations may occur when the Mg interlayer thickness is higher
than 0.6 nm.
However, there are differences in the annealing dependence of
the TMR ratio in p-MTJs depending on the Mg interlayer thickness.
Although the TMR effects deteriorate at an annealing temperature
close to 300 ○C for all p-MTJs, the reduction in the TMR ratio is
moderate at Mg interlayer thicknesses of 0.2, 0.4, and 0.5 nm, as
compared to that at 0, 0.6, and 0.8 nm. These observations suggest
that the Mg interlayer with thicknesses of 0.2–0.5 nm modifies the
nano/atomic structures and thermal endurance of the bcc CoMn
interlayer and interfaces.
Figure 2(a) shows the cross-sectional structural analysis via
HAADF-STEM for p-MTJs annealed at 260 ○C. The (001)-oriented
bcc CoMn layer and the (001)-oriented MgO barrier have a rela-
tively flat interface at CoMn/MgO. All layers are epitaxially grown,
as suggested by the coherence of the lattice fringes. Figures 2(b) and
2(c) show the line profiles of EDX for the as-prepared MTJ and
MTJ annealed at 260 ○C, respectively. For the as-prepared MTJ, the
presence of Mg can be seen as a weak and broad EDX peak at the
interface of CoMn and MnGa [Fig. 2(b)]. However, several line pro-
file measurements indicated that the Mg interlayer was not spatially
homogeneous (not shown here). Therefore, the Mg interlayer may
be present at the interface as islands or discontinuous layers. For
the annealed MTJs, the EDX peak of Mg is no longer visible at the
CoMn and MnGa interfaces [Fig. 2(c)], indicating that Mg atoms
are completely diffused during the annealing process. In addition,
the Mn and Co concentrations increased near the interface of the
MgO barrier and in the MnGa layer, respectively, after annealing, as
shown in Figs. 2(b) and 2(c). Therefore, the atomic diffusions of sev-
eral types of elements may deteriorate the TMR effect at annealing
temperatures closer to 300 ○C [Fig. 1(b)].
Figures 3(a)–3(c) show the MR curves for the p-MTJs with dif-
ferent thicknesses of CoMn layers, measured at 300 K by applying a
magnetic field of up to 90 kOe. The MTJ with tCoMn = 1.0 nm has the
highest TMR ratio in this study: 118% at 300 K and more than 260%
at 10 K, as shown in Fig. 3(d).

temperature dependence of the TMR effect and junction resis-
tance in terms of elastic, in-elastic, and defect-mediated tunnel-
ing transport.46–48 In general, the temperature dependence of RAp
becomes weaker with decreasing numbers of defects in the bar-
rier.46 The reduction in such imperfections may also reduce dif-
fuse electron scattering/tunneling and promote coherent tunneling,
enhancing the TMR effect relevant to the highly spin-polarized Δ1
Bloch bands in bcc CoMn and bcc FeCo(B).36,37,49,50 In addition,
the enhanced coherent tunneling can be caused by reductions in
electrode oxidization at the interface of the MgO barrier.51 These

IV. SUMMARY
We studied p-MTJs comprising perpendicularly magnetized
MnGa and FeCoB electrodes using thin metastable bcc CoMn alloys
as an interlayer between MnGa and MgO and a thin Mg interlayer
between MnGa and CoMn. We observed a TMR ratio exceeding
100% when the Mg interlayer thicknesses were 0.4 and 0.5 nm. Such
an ultrathin Mg interlayer did not disturb the epitaxial growth of
metastable bcc CoMn(001) on MnGa(001) from the detailed TEM
analysis. We discussed the enhanced TMR effect in terms of the
enhanced coherent tunneling by introducing the Mg interlayer. This
finding may be helpful for the further development of p-MTJs with
Mn-based tetragonal alloys for various potential applications.
