ABSTRACT
We investigated the tunnel magnetoresistance (TMR) effect in fully perpendicular magnetized MnGa-based magnetic tunnel junctions (MTJs)
with CoFeB as the top electrode. In these perpendicular (p)-MTJs, thin metastable body-centered cubic (bcc) CoMnFe alloys are used as an
interlayer between the MgO barrier and MnGa layers to enhance the TMR ratio. This study highlights the utility of bcc Co-based alloys,
such as CoMnFe, which exhibit antiferromagnetic coupling with MnGa in p-MTJs. The TMR ratios of ∼106% and 110% were observed
corresponding to single and double interface p-MTJs, respectively, after annealing at 300 ○C, demonstrating the potential of these materials
for high-performance spin-transfer torque (STT) devices based on p-MTJs.

I. INTRODUCTION
Tetragonal Mn-based ordered magnetic alloys have attracted
attention because of their unique magnetic properties.1,2 Past stud-
ies discussed about their large uniaxial magnetic anisotropy, small
net magnetization, and relatively low Gilbert damping constants
observed in L10-MnZ (Z = Al, Ga), D022-Mn3Z (Z = Al, Ga, Ge,
Sn), and C38-MnYZ (Y = Al, Ga, Mn, Z = Ge, Sb).1–6 Those (001)-
oriented films exhibit strong perpendicular anisotropy (PMA),
therefore those films are well suited for magnetic electrodes in
perpendicular (p-) magnetic tunnel junctions (MTJs). The p-MTJs
are used for spin-transfer-torque magnetoresistive memory (STT-
MRAM) and also for potential logic, high-frequency applications,
and artificial intelligence hardware.7–12 Therefore, there have been
many studies focusing on those thin films for such STT-device
applications.13–27
One issue of p-MTJs using Mn-based ordered magnetic alloys
has been their low tunnel magnetoresistance (TMR) effect. The TMR
effect is used for memory reading in STT-MRAM, for examples,
therefore the TMR ratio should be large, at least, more than 100%
for practical use. However, such high TMR effects had not been
experimentally observed,28–30 though ab initio calculations had indi-
cated high TMR ratios in the p-MTJ comprising a (001)-oriented
MgO barrier and Mn-based alloy electrodes.31 This may be par-
tially due to the difficulty obtaining a better crystalline interface in
the Mn-based alloys (001)/MgO(001) barrier with some lattice mis-
match. Although there had also been many attempts to obtain a high
TMR ratio using an interlayer technique, such as in Mn-based alloys
(001)/FeCo(B)(001)/MgO(001) barrier,32–36 the TMR ratio had not
yet exceeded several tens of percent. The reason is unclear because
the high TMR ratio above 100%, typically obtained in p-MTJ with
a MgO(001) barrier and FeCo(B)(001) electrodes37,38 and also a

Quite recently, we resolved this issue of the low TMR ratio in
Mn-based alloys p-MTJs utilizing novel interlayer magnetic alloys.
We first reported a TMR ratio of 85% in p-MTJs with a metastable
bcc CoMn(001) between MnGa and MgO,46 Subsequently, the TMR
ratio more than 100% was successfully demonstrated in further
optimized MTJs with CoMn interlayers.47 This breakthrough is
brought into via intriguing metastable bcc CoMn alloys.48–51 How-
ever, questions and issues are remained. One issue is that the TMR
ratio for those MTJs becomes deteriorated at the MTJs annealing
temperature about 250 ○C, and this is related to a low thermal sta-
bility of metastable bcc structure of CoMn interlayer, as discussed
before.46,47,49,52 In general, the high annealing stability is needed
since the TMR effect tends to increase with increasing annealing
temperature.41
To address this annealing stability issue and gain insight into
the mechanism behind it, we investigate the interlayer of metastable
bcc CoMnFe alloys. As demonstrated in our previous study,53 the in-
plane magnetized MTJs with bcc CoMnFe alloys electrodes showed
better stability against the annealing process in their bcc structure
up to 400 ○C and also exhibit the TMR ratio much higher than those
in the MTJs with CoMn alloy electrodes, up to about 350% at room
temperature and more than 1000% at low temperature. This indi-
cates that it may be effective to apply this CoMnFe interlayer to the
Mn-based alloys p-MTJs. Consequently, using bcc metastable CoM-
nFe interlayer, we successfully achieved both the TMR ratio of more
than 100% as well as the annealing stability at the temperature of
more than 300 ○C, as shown in this article.
II. EXPERIMENTAL METHODS
Sample films were deposited using an ultrahigh-vacuum mag-
netron sputtering system with a base pressure below 5 × 10−7 Pa.
We prepared two kinds of MTJ. One is the MTJ with a top CoFeB
layer as a conventional MTJ, whose stack was MgO(001) substrate
Cr(40)/MnGa(30)/Mg(0.4)/CoMnFe(tCoMnFe)/Mg(0.4)/MgO(dMgO)/
CoFeB(1.2)/Ta(3)/Ru(5) with various thickness of CoMnFe tCoMnFe
= 1.0, 1.2, and 1.4 nm and MgO barrier dMgO = 1.6, 1.8, 2.0, 2.2, and
2.4 nm. We also fabricated the MTJs with a top CoFeB/Ta/
CoFeB
layer,
the
so-called
double
interface.54,55
The
stack
was MgO(001) substrate/Cr(40)/MnGa(30)/Mg(0.4)/CoMnFe(1.2)/
Mg(0.4)/MgO(2)/CoFeB(1.4)/Ta(0.4)/CoFeB(1.2)/MgO(1.4)/Ta(3)/
Ru(5). Here, the numbers written in parentheses are in nanometers.
We used the alloy targets of Mn55 Ga45, Co60 Mn23 Fe17, and Co20
Fe60 B20 (at%). The compositions of MnGa and CoMnFe films were
Mn61 Ga39 and Co65 Mn19 Fe16 (at. %), evaluated with inductively-
coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) in those alloy films
prepared separately. In all the stacks, the substrate was flushed at
700 ○C before the deposition for cleaning their surfaces. Then, each
layer was deposited at room temperature. In situ post-annealing was
performed for Cr and MnGa at 700 and 500 ○C, respectively. The
magnetic properties were investigated using the polar magneto-
optical Kerr effect (p-MOKE) with the out-of-plane magnetic
field range of ±20 kOe at room temperature. We performed the
MTJ patterning using standard optical lithography technique with
Ar-ion etching, and the junction area, A, for the MTJs ranged from
10 × 10 μm2 to 100 × 100 μm2. MTJs were annealed using a vacuum
furnace, and magnetoresistance measurements were performed
using the standard four-probe method at room temperature with
the out-of-plane magnetic field range of ±5 kOe.
III. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS
To investigate the magnetization behavior of MnGa and CoM-
nFe, we performed p-MOKE measurements for multilayer films
[Fig. 1(a)]. Figures 1(b)–1(e) show the magnetic field dependence
of the p-MOKE signal for the as-deposited films and for the
films annealed at different annealing temperatures (Ta). Here, we
depicted the individual magnetization directions for the MnGa and
CoMnFe magnetic layers in the figures. For reference, we also show
Ta dependence of the Kerr rotation angles evaluated at 20 kOe and
zero field in Fig. 1(f).
In the as-deposited film, the p-MOKE curve shows a hystere-
sis loop with a slope between the magnetic field range of ±5 kOe
[Fig. 1(b)]. We observe that the remanent Kerr rotation angle, corre-
sponding to the remanent magnetization (the point “2”), is slightly
higher than that of the MnGa single-layer film (not shown here).
This indicates that the magnetization of CoMnFe is coupled to
MnGa through the interfacial ferromagnetic coupling and the mag-
netization of CoMnFe is tilted slightly upward at zero field.56 Note
that the direction of the magnetization for CoMnFe becomes like
a cone state at a certain point, as shown in Figs. 1(b)–1(e), rather
than tilt to the left or right direction. This behavior arises because
CoMnFe and MnGa layers exhibit different magnetic anisotropies:
CoMnFe exhibits easy-plane magnetic anisotropy, whereas MnGa
shows perpendicular magnetic anisotropy. With taking account of
the interfacial ferromagnetic coupling, the magnetization process for
the as-deposited film can be understood as follows. At the field
of 20 kOe, the magnetizations of MnGa and CoMnFe are aligned
in the same direction (the point “1”) and display saturation type
behavior above ±10 kOe. As the magnetic field was swept from
20 kOe to −20 kOe, the magnetization of CoMnFe starts to rotate
from the upward to the downward direction. Below 10 kOe, the
Kerr rotation angle decreases approximately linearly and shows a
lower magnetization value compared to the high-field condition at
the point “2.” At the magnetic field of −20 kOe, the magnetiza-
tions of both layers are aligned parallel but in the opposite direction
(the point “3”).
On the other hand, we can understand the magnetization
process for the annealed films with taking account of the interfa-
cial antiferromagnetic coupling for the magnetization of MnGa and
CoMnFe [Figs. 1(c)–1(e)]. For the sample annealed at 250 ○C, the
hysteresis loop exhibits a non-saturating behavior throughout the
magnetic field range of ±20 kOe [Fig. 1(c)]. The Kerr rotation angle
at 20 kOe is close to that observed in the as-deposited films, and the
remanent Kerr rotation angle is lower than that of the as-deposited
films as well as the MnGa single-layer film. These data suggest a weak
antiferromagnetic coupling between the layers, as mentioned above.
Upon annealing at 300 ○C, the film shows a fully-squared hysteresis
loop within the magnetic field range of ±5 kOe and displays non-
saturating behavior above ±5 kOe [Fig. 1(d)]. The value of remanent
Kerr rotation angle further decreases in the 300 ○C-annealed film,
as compared to that for the 250 ○C-annealed film, which indicates
that the antiferromagnetic coupling between MnGa and CoMnFe
becomes moderate via the annealing. A further increase in the

Figure 2(a) shows the schematic representation of the stack
information for p-MTJs prepared in the present study, and
Figs. 2(b)–2(e) show the magnetoresistance curve for the as-

ness is 1.2 nm and the barrier thickness of 2.0 nm. In Figs. 2(b)–2(e),
we evaluated the TMR ratio using the relation TMR (%) = (Rmax
−Rmin)/Rmin × 100, where Rmin and Rmax represent the MTJ resis-
tance related to the parallel and antiparallel states of the magnetiza-
tion directions for CoFeB and CoMnFe. Note that we schematically
show the magnetization state at certain magnetic field in its sweep
range of −5 kOe to +5 kOe in Figs. 2(b)–2(e).
In Fig. 2(b), the TMR curve shows a clear change in resis-
tance from low to high and vice versa as the magnetic field was
swept from ±5 kOe to ∓5 kOe. The characteristic of the TMR curve
observed here is similar to that for a typical pseudo spin-valve p-
MTJs,32,58 which reflects the magnetization process for CoFeB and
MnGa/CoMnFe with the ferromagnetic coupling, as discussed in
Fig. 1(b). On the other hand, the TMR curves show inverted hys-
teresis loops for the MTJs annealed, as observed in Figs. 2(c)–2(e).
These inverted TMR curves are caused by the magnetization process
for CoFeB and MnGa/CoMnFe with the antiferromagnetic coupling,
as discussed in Figs. 1(c)–1(e).
One can understand the shape of the TMR curves in
Figs. 2(c)–2(e) as follows. For example, we consider the magneti-
zation process in the magnetic field range from −5 kOe to +5 kOe
(red solid symbols) in the Fig. 2(d). At the magnetic field of −5 kOe
(the point “i”), the magnetic field is not strong at all enough to align
the magnetizations for all the layers in the same direction due to
the moderate antiferromagnetic coupling, as discussed in Fig. 1(d).
The magnetization for CoMnFe is slightly tilted and approximately

TMR curve shape for the MTJs annealed at different temperatures,

increasing the annealing temperature in the high resistance state

To gain insight into the mechanism determining the TMR ratio
observed, we also investigated the influence of the MgO barrier
thickness to the TMR ratio. Figures 4(a) and 4(b) show the TMR
ratio and RPA as the function of the MgO barrier thickness (dMgO)
for different Ta with tCoMnFe = 1.2 nm, respectively. As the MgO
thickness increases, the value of the TMR ratio increases slightly
up to 2 nm and is then almost saturated. We observed the high-
est TMR ratio of 106% for 2 nm thick MgO barrier at Ta = 300 ○C.
RPA almost exponentially increases with increasing the barrier
thickness. These features are similar to those observed in the MTJs
such as Fe/MgO/Fe, stemming from the coherent tunneling in
CoMnFe/MgO/CoFe(B) structures, namely the TMR ratio is mainly
governed by the coherent tunneling of fully spin-polarized Δ1 band
between CoFe(B)(001) and CoMnFe(001) across the MgO(001)
barrier.60
Finally, we also investigated the p-MTJs with the top
CoFeB/Ta/CoFeB/MgO to understand the effect of CoFeB thick-
ness on the TMR ratio in the present p-MTJs. This is because
the coherent tunneling TMR effect becomes small in p-MTJs
with very thin CoFeB electrodes, as previously discussed.61 This
CoFeB/Ta/CoFeB/MgO p-MTJs stack allows us to increase the
CoFeB thickness while maintaining perpendicular magnetization
due to the interfacial PMA stemming from the top MgO layer.62
However, a stronger PMA generally causes a larger current for STT
switching in p-MTJs devices,63–67 which needs to be addressed for
practical applications.

From the above-mentioned experimental results, it is likely
that the reduction of the TMR ratio at 325 ○C may be related
to the deterioration of the bottom layer consisting of MnGa and
CoMnFe. We speculate that the deterioration of the TMR ratio
is caused by the small amount of the thermal diffusion of Ga
from MnGa into CoMnFe. The Ga diffusion was also discussed
in MnGa/CoMn interface in the transmission electron micro-
scopy experiments.52 Moreover, we have observed the TMR ratio
of less than 100% in p-MTJs without the ultrathin Mg layer
between MnGa and CoMnFe in the present study (not shown here).
As discussed previously, the ultrathin Mg insertion layer serves a role
in obtaining a better interface between MnGa layer and CoMn.47
This may be also the case in the present MTJs, where the ultra-
thin Mg layer likely suppresses the Ga diffusion at Ta lower than
300–325 ○C.
In summary, we investigated the p-MTJs with the MnGa elec-
trode utilizing the metastable bcc CoMnFe alloys interlayer. We
successfully achieved both the TMR ratio of more than 100% as well
as the annealing stability at the temperature up to 300 ○C, demon-
strating the advances of p-MTJs with the Mn-based tetragonal alloys
toward STT devices with better performance. We also investigated
the p-MTJs with the top CoFeB/Ta/CoFeB/MgO electrodes to gain
insight into the mechanism of the deterioration of the TMR ratio
after the annealing. For further enhancing the TMR ratio as well
as annealing stability, it is necessary to explore the way to suppress
atomic diffusion across the interface of MnGa/CoMnFe.
