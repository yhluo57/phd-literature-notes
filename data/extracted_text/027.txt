ABSTRACT
We investigate the magnetic properties and spin-orbit-torque-induced (SOT-induced) magnetization switching in Ta/MnGa/Cr and
Ta/MnGa/NiAl structures. Out-of-plane hysteresis loops for the Ta/MnGa/Cr (NiAl) structures are skewed (square). In-plane currents I
are applied to Hall devices made of structures exposed to in-plane magnetic fields Hin along the channel direction. Clear asymmetric mag-
netization switching with respect to the polarity of I and Hin is detected for the Ta/MnGa/NiAl device. Similar asymmetric magnetization
switching is observed for the Ta/MnGa/Cr device, although the magnetization is partially switched regardless of the polarity of I and Hin.
These results suggest that an in-plane magnetization component opposite to Hin is present in the Cr-buffer structure and that the formation
of such a component is suppressed in the NiAl-buffer structure.

I. INTRODUCTION
Spintronics devices using magnetization reversal by spin-
orbit torques (SOTs) have attracted significant attention for next-
generation electronics. A number of studies on magnetization rever-
sal by SOTs have been reported in metallic magnetic heterostruc-
tures consisting of a ferromagnetic layer and a heavy metal (HM)
layer.1–5 In most studies on SOTs, the SOT originating from spin
current generated by the spin Hall effect (SHE) in the HM layer is
used to manipulate the magnetization direction, and perpendicu-
lar magnetization is switched by applying in-plane current under a
magnetic field along the current direction2–5 because high-speed and
efficient electrical manipulation of the magnetization direction by
SOT has been demonstrated in systems with perpendicular magnetic
anisotropy (PMA).6
Perpendicularly magnetized MnGa is promising as the fer-
romagnetic layer for spintronics devices using SOT-induced
magnetization reversal because it has relatively large PMA and
low magnetization.7–9 Ultrathin MnGa films are desirable for such
spintronics applications, although the PMA reportedly deteriorates
with decreasing thickness.10–12 Material candidates for buffer lay-
ers deposited under MnGa may significantly affect the PMA,13 and
ultrathin (1–5 nm) MnGa films with PMA have been fabricated
by using CoGa buffer layers.14 SOTs and SOT-induced magnetiza-
tion switching have also been reported in perpendicularly magne-
tized HM/MnGa and MnGa/CoGa structures with a MnGa thick-
ness ≥2 nm.15–20 Recently, we fabricated perpendicularly mag-
netized 1-nm-thick MnGa film by using a NiAl buffer layer and
demonstrated clear SOT-induced magnetization switching.21 In the
present work, we examine the magnetic properties and SOT-induced
magnetization switching in Ta/MnGa (1–3 nm) structures grown
on Cr and NiAl buffer layers to investigate how deteriorated
PMA affects the SOT-induced magnetization switching in mag-
netic heterostructures based on MnGa. Answering this question is

II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
All samples were deposited on a (001) MgO single-crystal
substrate at room temperature. We prepared two sets of sam-
ples: Cr-buffer structures (samples A and B) and NiAl-buffer
structures (samples C and D). Samples A and B consisted of
MgO(2)/Ta(5)/MnGa(3)/Cr(3) from the surface side, where the
numbers in parentheses are nominal thicknesses in nanome-
ters. Samples C and D consisted of MgO(2)/Ta(5)/MnGa(2 and
1)/NiAl(3), respectively. The compositions of NiAl and MnGa were
determined to be Ni1.1Al1, Mn1.0Ga1 for sample A and Mn1.4Ga1 for
samples B–D by using inductively coupled plasma optical-emission
spectroscopy. The Cr (NiAl) buffer layer was deposited by using
electron-beam evaporation (magnetron sputtering), and both buffer
layers were annealed at 540 ○C in a vacuum after the deposition.
The MnGa layer for sample A (samples B–D) was deposited by
using molecular beam epitaxy (magnetron sputtering), and the layer
was annealed at 400 ○C in a vacuum after deposition. Following
the deposition of the MnGa layer, the Ta and MgO layers were
deposited by using magnetron sputtering and electron-beam evap-
oration, respectively, where the top MgO layer served as a capping
layer to prevent oxidization of the Ta layer. By conventional pho-
tolithography and Ar ion milling, some of samples were processed
into Hall devices with a 10-μm-wide channel and a pair of Hall
probes to examine SOT-induced magnetization switching.
III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Figures 1(a)–1(c) [1(d)–1(f)] show reflection high-energy elec-
tron diffraction (RHEED) patterns for the annealed Cr (NiAl) buffer
layer, the as-deposited MnGa layer, and the annealed MnGa layer
in sample B (D), respectively. After annealing, both buffer layers
exhibited streak RHEED patterns [Figs. 1(a) and 1(d)], which indi-
cates that both the layers grew epitaxially on the MgO substrate. The
RHEED pattern for the as-deposited MnGa layer on the Cr buffer
layer is a broken streak pattern [Fig. 1(b)], whereas the pattern for
the NiAl buffer layer is a streak pattern [Fig. 1(e)]. After the anneal-
ing, both MnGa layers exhibited streak RHEED patterns [Figs. 1(c)
and 1(f)], which indicates epitaxial growth of the MnGa layers on
both buffer layers. The Mn composition of the MnGa used in this
study and the streak RHEED patterns suggest that MnGa has a L10
structure.22 Based on a halo RHEED pattern acquired after Ta depo-
sition on the MnGa layers, all Ta layers are amorphous. To exam-
ine the crystal structure of both buffer layers, we performed θ-2θ
x-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements with Cu Kα and Kβ radia-
tion for separately prepared buffer layers under the same conditions
(see above). Figures 1(g) and 1(h) show the XRD patterns for the
Cr(30) and NiAl(30) layers, respectively. The diffraction peak of the
Cr layer is consistent with that of bcc Cr.10,12,13 For the NiAl layer,
two diffraction peaks appear, which are consistent with those for
CsCl-type (B2)-NiAl.23,24

Ryx for samples A–D as a function of the out-of-plane magnetic
field μ0H (μ0 is the permeability of vacuum) at room temperature,

buffer structures (samples A and B) showed skewed hysteresis loops
[Figs. 2(a) and 2(b)] regardless of the Mn composition (Mn1.0Ga1
or Mn1.4Ga1) and the deposition techniques (molecular beam epi-
taxy or magnetron sputtering). Note that reducing MnGa thickness
(<3 nm) further deteriorates the PMA in the Cr buffer. The skewed
hysteresis loops suggest that the PMA is insufficient to orient the
magnetization direction out of the plane and that in-plane magne-
tization components are present in the Cr-buffer structures. The
NiAl-buffer structures (samples C and D), however, have square
hysteresis loops even for the sample with a 1-nm-thick MnGa layer.
We confirmed that the NiAl layer is nonmagnetic at room tempera-
ture by using the magneto-optical Kerr effect measurement,21 which
means that the observed hysteresis loops originate from the MnGa
layer. From the magnetization measurement, saturation magnetiza-
tion for the 1-nm-thick MnGa structure was determined to be 0.75
T, which is comparable to that for MnGa film grown on MgO.25
These results indicate that the addition of the NiAl layer creates
perpendicular magnetized ultrathin MnGa, as reported previously.21

Early experiments showed that the PMA of the MnGa layer is signif-
icantly affected by the lattice distortion of MnGa.7,10–14 Because the
surface roughness of the as-deposited MnGa layer on the Cr buffer
exceeds that of the NiAl layer [based on RHEED patterns; Figs. 1(b)
and 1(e)], the deteriorated PMA observed in the Cr-buffer samples
can be related to local lattice distortions at the MnGa–buffer-layer
interface.
To understand how the deterioration of the PMA affects the
magnetization switching, we examined SOT-induced magnetization
switching for samples A (Cr buffer) and D (NiAl buffer). An out-of-
plane magnetic field of μ0H = −0.5 T was first applied to the Hall
devices, and then the field was removed so that μ0H = 0, where a
positive (negative) H is defined as a magnetic field pointing into
(out of) the plane. Next, an in-plane magnetic field Hin along the
channel direction was applied to the devices (initial state). Subse-
quently, a 1 s current pulse I of varying amplitude was applied to
the channel. After the application of each current pulse I, we mea-
sured Ryx to monitor the magnetization direction with a low current
of 100 μA. Figures 3(a) and 3(c) [3(b) and 3(d)] show the normal-
ized Ryx as a function of negative (positive) I under μ0Hin = 0.1 T
and −0.1 T, respectively, for samples A (Cr buffer) and D (NiAl
buffer). A positive (negative) I and Hin are defined as the current
and magnetic field pointing left-to-right (right-to-left) in the chan-
nel direction, respectively. When I and Hin are parallel (antiparallel),
clear (almost no) magnetization switching from -z to +z is detected
for the NiAl-buffer sample. Suppose spin current generating from

y) direction for positive (negative) I.2 When I and Hin are parallel
(antiparallel), the spin moments exert effective magnetic field point-
ing in the +z (-z) direction on magnetization. Therefore, this clear

Assuming that the SHE in the Ta layer is the source of the SOT, the
switching current density is one order of magnitude less than that
expected from the macrospin model.26 This can be related to mag-
netization reversal through SOT-induced chiral domain motion, as
suggested for the same MnGa-based heterostructure.21 A similar
asymmetric magnetization switching was detected in the Cr-buffer
sample, although the magnetization was partially switched regard-
less of the polarity of I and Hin. The asymmetric magnetization
switching indicates that the SOT originating from the SHE in the Ta
layer plays a critical role in the switching. In view of the skewed out-
of-plane hysteresis loops detected in the Cr-buffer sample, we specu-
late that an in-plane magnetization component oriented opposite to
Hin is present even under Hin and the partial switching is caused by
the in-plane component in the Cr-buffer sample. Indeed, in-plane
remanent magnetization was confirmed from in-plane magnetiza-
tion measurements for a separately prepared 15-nm thick MnGa
grown on Cr buffer (not shown). Conversely, for the NiAl sam-
ple, the formation of such an in-plane magnetization component
is suppressed and clear SOT-induced magnetization switching is
detected.
IV. CONCLUSIONS
We investigate the magnetic properties and SOT-induced mag-
netization switching in Ta/MnGa (1–3 nm) structures grown on
Cr and NiAl buffer layers. Out-of-plane hysteresis loops for the
Ta/MnGa/Cr (NiAl) structures are skewed (square), and the RHEED
patterns for the as-deposited MnGa layer on the Cr- and NiAl-
buffer layers suggest that the surface roughness of the as-deposited
MnGa layer on the Cr buffer exceeds that on the NiAl buffer, and
