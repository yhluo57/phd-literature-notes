1.  Introduction
Magnetic field sensors have attracted much interest in the last 
few decades for their potential applications in the fields of 
weak magnetic detection and medical inspection, as well as 
automotive systems, due to their high sensitivity, low power 
consumption and long endurance [1]. Currently, Hall sensors 
are widely used in these areas, especially for high magnetic 
field sensing with high spatial resolution. However, the Hall 
sensors have some shortcomings, such as poor thermal sta­
bility and a limited operation frequency range, which restricts 
their wide applications. Recently, the magnetoresistance (MR) 
sensors based on spin valves and magnetic tunnel junctions 
have become the new generation magnetic field sensors for 
their higher sensitivity and smaller size, compared with tradi­
tional Hall effect sensors and anisotropic magnetoresistance 
(AMR) sensors [2–4]. In MR sensors, the basic concerns are 
to realize a linear and reversible response to the applied magn­
etic field, high sensitivity and large dynamic range [5, 6]. To 
date, there are two main methods to align the magnetizations 
of the sensing layer and the reference layer to be initially 
perpendicular to each other. One is utilizing the shape aniso­
tropy and step edge anisotropy in a film plane, or introducing 
a transverse bias field, which complicates the system design 
and leads to the magnetic noise. The other approach is uti­
lizing out-of-plane magnetized materials to realize the vertical 
alignment. In this configuration, the magnetization of the ref­
erence and sensing layers is aligned along the out-of-plane 
and in-plane directions, respectively [7, 8], which has been 
widely used in recent designs of tunneling magnetoresist­
ance (TMR) sensors as an alternative to Hall sensors for high 
magn­etic field sensing with high spatial resolution.
Perpendicular field sensors based on the TMR effect have 
been intensely investigated for their promising applications 
in mobile devices. Generally, to realize the linear response to 
the applied field and large dynamic range in the TMR effect 
based perpendicular magnetic field sensors, the ferromagnetic 
material acting as a reference layer requires a high coercive 
force (Hc), a high spin polarization and well matching with 
MgO barriers. In recent years, several kinds of materials with 
perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA) have been incor­
porated into MgO based MTJ field sensors, such as CoFeB,

Abstract
We report on the investigation of the magnetic tunnel junction structure designed for high 
magnetic field sensors with a perpendicularly magnetized L10-MnGa reference layer and an 
in-plane magnetized Fe sensing layer. A large linear tunneling magnetoresistance ratio up 
to 27.4% and huge dynamic range up to 5600 Oe have been observed at 300 K, with a low 
nonlinearity of 0.23% in the optimized magnetic tunnel junction (MTJ). The field response of 
tunneling magnetoresistance is discussed to explain the field sensing properties in the dynamic 
range. These results indicate that L10-MnGa based orthogonal MTJ is a promising candidate 
for a high performance magnetic field sensor with a large dynamic range, high endurance and 
low power consumption.
Keywords: magnetic field sensor, magnetic tunnel junctions, dynamic range, molecular-beam 
epitaxy, L10-MnGa

[Co/Pt(Pd)]n and Pt/CoFe [8–15]. However, CoFeB based 
MTJ field sensors show large and linear TMR signals at low 
magn­etic field, usually no more than 150 Oe, due to the small 
coercivity. In the case of the [Co/Pt(Pd)]n based MTJ sensor, 
the linear dynamic range has been extended but still no more 
than 2500 Oe [15]. Meanwhile, a small TMR ratio was always 
observed in [Co/Pt(Pd)]n and Pt/CoFe based MTJs, which is 
attributed to the low spin polarization.
Among the various PMA materials, tetragonal L10-MnGa 
alloys have shown many attractive properties, such as large 
PMA (Ku ~ 107 erg cm−3) [16, 17], a low Gilbert damping 
constant of 0.008, and a high spin polarization of transport 
electrons of more than 70% [18, 19]. These properties make 
the tetragonal L10-MnGa alloy an excellent candidate for ultra­
high-density magnetic recording media, spin transfer-torque 
MR random access memory (STT-MRAM) with low critical 
current density and high power spin-torque nano-oscillators 
(STNO). Compared with the PMA materials mentioned above, 
L10-MnGa film exhibits a larger coercive field Hc and perpend­
icular magnetic anisotropy Ku, which is expected to facilitate 
out-of-plane field sensing in an extended dynamic range and 
keep thermal stability. In previous works, a relatively small 
TMR ratio was obtained in L10-MnGa based MTJs, due to 
the large lattice mismatch (7%) between the L10-MnGa and 
MgO barrier layers. To solve this problem, the magnetic layer/
barrier interfaces were usually modified by inserting a con­
ventional 3D transition metal (typically Fe, Co) alloys layer to 
increase the TMR ratio [20–22].
In this work, we investigated the TMR effect in 
L10-Mn1.5Ga/Co/MgO/Fe MTJs field sensors with an orthog­
onal alignment of magnetization. The measured TMR ratio of 
the MTJ device is up to 27.4% with the Co thickness of 0.8 nm 
at 300 K. In particular, a large dynamic range up to 5600 Oe 
was observed in our devices with ultra-small nonlinearity and 
excellent reproducibility. Finally, the mechanism of field sen­
sitivity and the nonlinearity properties of the MTJ device were 
analyzed in detail.
2.  Experiments
The MTJ structure consisting of a L10-MnGa(25)/Co(0.8)/
MgO(2.3)/Fe(5)/Pd(2) (thickness unit: nanometers) multilayer 
was deposited on a GaAs (0 0 1) substrate by a molecular-
beam epitaxy (MBE) system with two growth chambers. A 
150-nm-thick GaAs buffer layer was first epitaxially grown on 
the GaAs (0 0 1) substrate to smooth the surface. Subsequently, 
without breaking the vacuum, the sample was transferred via 
an ultrahigh vacuum channel to the second growth chamber. 
A 25-nm-thick L10-Mn1.5Ga film was then epitaxially grown 
on the GaAs buffer layer at 250 °C, followed by an in situ 
annealing process at 300 °C to improve the L10 phase order 
and decrease the surface roughness. The Co layer was inserted 
between the L10-MnGa and MgO barrier layers with a thick­
ness of 0.8 nm, which is an optimized value to suppress the 
defects arising from the lattice mismatch [22]. The MgO bar­
rier layer was subsequently deposited using the electron beam 
evaporation source at room temperature.
The films were patterned into pillar shapes with sizes 
ranging from 10  ×  10 µm2 to 70  ×  70 µm2 using typical ultra­
violet lithography and Ar plasma milling techniques. After the 
fabrication processes, the MTJ devices were annealed in a 
vacuum furnace with high vacuum at 300 °C under a magn­
etic field of 5000 Oe for 30 min. The crystalline and inter­
face characteristics of a MTJ multilayer were confirmed by 
cross-sectional high-resolution transmission electron micro­
scope (HRTEM) images. The static magnetic properties of the 
entire films were measured by the superconducting quantum 
interference device (SQUID) with a maximum applied field 
of 5 T. Electrical transport properties of the MTJ sensors were 
measured by a DC four-terminal method from 5 K to 300 K 
using a physical property measurement system (PPMS). The 
experimental results for the devices with the different sizes 
showed similar behaviors and all data shown below are for 
40  ×  40 µm2 pillars.

3.2. TMR of L10-MnGa based MTJ device
Figure 2(a) shows the TMR curves of MTJ sensors with 
a 0.8 nm Co insertion layer measured at 5, 100, 200 and 
300 K, respectively. Here, the TMR ratio was calculated by 
the form­ula (R  −  RL)/RL  ×  100%, where RL represents the 
magneto-resistance of the low resistance state. The TMR ratio 
reaches 27.4% at 300 K and 74.6% at 5 K, respectively, which 
are higher than the previously reported results in L10-MnGa/
FM/MgO/CoFe(Fe) based MTJs [20–22]. In L10-MnGa/MgO 
based MTJs, the inelastic scattering process induced by the 
large mismatch between MgO and MnGa (~7%) and diffu­
sion of the Mn element may be the reason for the low MR 
ratio. The benefits of the Co insertion layer are improving the 
lattice matching between the alloy and MgO barrier, as well 
as the high spin polarization of the ∆1 band [25]. The strong 
temper­ature dependence of TMR is attributed to the suppres­
sion of spin-flip scattering through tunneling in the MgO bar­
rier layer and stimulation of the impurity band around the 
Fermi level. In the spin-dependent scattering, the MgO barrier 
layer is the key to obtain a high TMR ratio in the MTJ, due to 
the spin filter effect [26]. Besides, due to the antiferromagn­
etic coupling between L10-MnGa and Co insertion layers 
[27, 28], the magnetization of the thin Co film is antiparallel 
to that of the L10-MnGa layer, which results in the high resist­
ance state at the high magnetic field in the TMR curves. The 
L10-MnGa layer and Co layer show coherent magnetization 
rotation behavior and thus work together as a reference layer. 
Therefore, the large coercive force of the Co layer induced by 
the anti-ferromagnetically coupled L10-MnGa provides a wide 
dynamic range for perpendicular field sensing. Moreover, the 
magnetization reversal behavior of each layer was discussed 
to gain a deep insight into the TMR curves of the MTJ field 
sensor device, which is shown in figure 2(b). For the reason 
that the magnetic field is aligned along the out-of-plane direc­
tion, the perpend­icular magnetization component of the soft 
magnetic layer Fe is proportional to the applied field H. Before 
a complete parallel state was achieved, the magnetization of 
the Co film had rotated to an opposite direction because of 
the spin-flip of the L10-MnGa reference layer. Therefore, the 
observed TMR ratio is probably underestimated because a 
complete parallel magnetization configuration in this struc­
ture is not achieved. In the L10-MnGa based MTJs with a thin 
Fe insertion layer, small shoulders appear in the TMR curves 
near the coercivity because of the relatively weak exchange 
coupling between the L10-MnGa and Fe [22, 23]. In our case, 
even when the applied field reaches 4 T, the MR curves keep 
at a high resist­ance state, which verifies the antiferromagnetic 
alignment of the magnetizations of the Co insertion layer and 
adjacent L10-MnGa reference layer. Thus, the interfacial anti­
ferromagnetic exchange coupling constant Jex (~M

3.3.  Perpendicular field sensing characteristics of L10-MnGa 
based MTJ device
To test the reproducibility of the MTJ device, we measured the 
R–H curves in the dynamic range from  −5600 Oe to  +5600 Oe 
under a perpendicular magnetic field at 300 K for 100 cycles. 
As shown in figure 3(a), the ten randomly selected cycles were 
stacked in the y-axis with a constant offset of 5 Ω. It is clear 
that the TMR of MTJ shows a linear response to the applied 
field H in the dynamic range. Besides, a good reversibility of 
R–H curves was demonstrated, due to the parallel arrangement 
of the randomly selected cycle curves (a good coincidence in 
the actual measurement). Throughout the 100 testing cycles, 
The TMR ratio (shown in the inset of figure  3(a)) defined 
in the dynamic range from  −5600 Oe to  +5600 Oe nearly 
keeps at the same value of 10.313% with the increasing cycle 
number. These results suggest a reversible magnetization 
rotation process of the sensing layer Fe, which is attributed to 
the single domain state in L10-MnGa based MTJ. Moreover, 
the field sensitivity is also a key factor for practical sensing 
applications. In our MTJ structure, the observed field sensi­
tivity is around 0.0011%/Oe, which is close to the value of 
commercialized semiconductor Hall sensors and large enough 
for reliable perpendicular magnetic field sensing.
As a crucial performance index in the practical application 
of field sensors, the nonlinearity of the first R–H cycle curve 
was analyzed in detail to demonstrate the field response char­
acteristic of the MTJ field sensor device. The nonlinearity, 
defined as 100%  ×  (R(H)  −  R0  −  r0H)/R(H) [7] is plotted in 
figure 3(b), where R0 and r0 are coefficients obtained from 
linear regression of TMR as a function of the applied magn­
etic field H. As shown in the figure 3(b), the measured TMR 
curve is almost coincided with linear fitted curve. A nonlin­
earity smaller than 0.23% is obtained in the dynamic range 
from  −5600 Oe to  +5600 Oe in the MTJ, which enables a 
precise sensing to the perpendicular magnetic field in a wide 
dynamic range.

4.  Conclusions
In summary, we have proposed a promising field sensor struc­
ture based on a L10-MnGa perpendicular anisotropy reference 
layer and an in-plane magnetized Fe layer. The insertion of 
a thin Co layer between the L10-MnGa and MgO barrier is 
an effective approach to improve the interface quality and 
enhance the MR ratio. The optimized MTJ sensors show a 
large TMR up to 27.4% at 300 K, which is higher than the 
previous results in L10-MnGa/FM/MgO/CoFe(Fe) based 
MTJs [22]. Meanwhile, the strong antiferromagnetic coupling 
between the L10-MnGa reference layer and the Co insertion 
layer has been observed. In particular, for perpendicular field 
sensing, a huge linear dynamic range up to 5600 Oe was 
achieved in our structure. The value is much larger than the 
previous results based on CoFeB, [Co/Pt(Pd)]n and Pt/CoFe. 
Moreover, an ultra-low nonlinearity and excellent reproduc­
ibility have been confirmed in the huge dynamic range. All 
of these results indicate that a L10-MnGa based orthogonal 
MTJ structure is a promising candidate for high performance 
magnetic field sensor.
